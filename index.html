Alarm Clock System - FPGA

ENSC 252 | May to July 2026

Project Parameters

Working with a partner, design a functional digital alarm clock on an FPGA board that could accept physical user inputs for configuring the clock and alarm while managing multiple operating states.

Design Process

I designed two finite state machines (FSMs) in VHDL, each with three different states. The first FSM handled the the timekeeping, with one state each for seconds, minutes, and hours. The second handled the operating modes, allowing the user to switch between clock, time configuration, and alarm configuration modes. I used one-hot state encoding for both FSMs to simplify the state transition logic and reduce the risk of glitches in the output.

Before deploying the design to the FPGA, I simulated the FSMs in ModelSim to verify that they correctly handled all possible input sequences and state transitions. My partner connected the physical switches and handled the remaining parts of the design, and together we integrated everything onto the Intel DE10 board.

Challenges

A major challenge was ensuring that the FSM correctly handled all possible input sequences, especially when multiple buttons were pressed in quick succession. One-hot encoding added to this, since every state transition and output had to be managed carefully to avoid glitches in the display and alarm behavior.

Result

Together, we combined the FSMs, input handling, real-time display, and alarm control into a single working FPGA-based system, earning a 98/100 project score.

What I Learned

Strengthened my understanding of FSM design and VHDL coding practice, and learned the importance of thorough simulation and hardware testing as they complement each other when debugging digital systems.

Hovercraft

Engineering Project | 2024

Project Parameters

Design and construct a functional hovercraft capable of generating stable lift, forward thrust, and directional control while meeting the project's physical and performance requirements.

Design Process

As a floater on a team of 4, I worked across the lift, thrust, and steering subsystems and took on a part of each, including frame design, prototyping, and system integration.

I calculated the lift, friction, frame dimensions, and center of mass to determine the structure and seat placement. I also designed and fabricated the wooden frame using AutoCAD, woodworking techniques, and power tools. The propulsion and lift system was based on a leaf blower, which we tuned through repeated testing to provide stable lift and forward thrust while maintaining control.

Challenges

Achieving stable lift and control was a challenge, as the hovercraft's weight distribution and propulsion system needed to be carefully balanced. Despite correct physics calculations, the hovercraft initially struggled to maintain stable lift and control, requiring iterative adjustments to the design and tuning of the propulsion system.

Coordination between different subteams also proved challenging at times, delaying progress on the project.

Result

The completed hovercraft integrated its mechanical structure, lift, propulsion, and steering systems into a functional vehicle.

What I Learned

Working across all three subsystems showed me how changes to one can affect the performance of the overall system, and how to coordinate with a team so that different components are compatible and work together effectively.

Touch-Activated Electronic Die

ENSC 120 | Sept. - Dec. 2025

Project Parameters

Build a touch-activated electronic die that lights a randomized 9-LED output when a user touches the sensor, with a gradual fade-out after the touch is released.

Design Process

Starting from a provided circuit design, I built the digital circuit and simulated it in LTspice to verify its behavior before assembly. I then assembled and soldered the resistors, capacitors, transistors, ICs, LEDs, and a copper touch sensor onto a circuit board, working from a provided manual and set of instructions.

After assembly, I tested the board and found issues with component placement that affected its performance. When the circuit did not behave as expected, I diagnosed the problem and corrected the placement of components to ensure reliable operation.

Challenges

Testing revealed placement and soldering issues that prevented the circuit from operating reliably. Only a few of the LEDs would light up when the sensor was touched, and the fade-out behavior was inconsistent.

I checked the soldered components against the circuit's behavior, traced the faulty behavior back to component placement, and corrected the affected connections before retesting.

Result

The finished board responded to touch with a randomized 9-LED output and a controlled fade-out, bringing together physical input, transistor and IC circuitry, and timing components. The project was completed with an A+.

What I Learned

I learned how small physical implementation details can significantly affect circuit performance, as well as how to troubleshoot and correct issues in a physical hardware build.

Digital Circuit Design LTSpice Integrated Circuits PCB Assembly and Soldering Hardware Debugging and Troubleshooting

Blackjack with Hi-Lo Counting

Personal Project | December 2025

Project Parameters

Develop an interactive Blackjack simulator in C++ with a built-in Hi-Lo card-counting trainer, while maintaining the state of a multi-hand game.

Design Process

I separated the program into independent classes for shoe management, hand logic, dealer rules, and player statistics. I implemented the standard actions of hit, stand, and double down, along with splitting up to 4 hands, insurance, blackjack payouts, soft hands, and split aces, with input validation for edge cases.

A betting and bank system tracks the player's balance and statistics across multiple rounds. I also built a Hi-Lo trainer that calculates the running and true counts, tracks the remaining card composition, and includes interactive quizzes for practicing counting.

Challenges

Managing the interactions between different game components required careful handling and proper organization of the game state. The most challenging features were splitting hands and insurance payouts, which required additional logic to handle edge cases so the game operated correctly.

Result

The completed program supports full Blackjack gameplay and tracks player balance and statistics over time, so different strategies can be evaluated across games. The Hi-Lo trainer adds interactive quizzes for practicing counting.

What I Learned

Strengthened my understanding of object-oriented programming principles in C++ and learned how to manage complex interactions between classes and game states by breaking them down into smaller, workable components.

C++ Object Oriented Programming Game Development Hi-Lo Card Counting Algorithm Design

FamFlavours Mobile App

Game of Apps Competition | 2024

Project Parameters

Design a mobile app prototype for the Game of Apps competition around the theme “Connecting Generations.”

Design Process

I'm Filipino, and food is the main way I know generations connect, so our team built a recipe-sharing app around that idea. The five of us split the design panels in Figma and worked through the user flows together, deciding how each button led to the next screen.

I built two panels centered on Filipino cuisine, designed the home section, and developed the app's color scheme and overall design. I also coordinated design tasks and tracked deliverables across the team from concept to final presentation. The result was a clickable Figma prototype across 10+ screens.

Challenges

With the panels split between five people, it was hard to keep separately designed screens consistent, and communication within the team was not always smooth. We often had different ideas about the direction and had to work through those disagreements before settling on one.

I helped keep the panels connected through the home design, the shared color scheme, and coordinating tasks across the team.

Result

We presented the interactive prototype in the final presentation and earned 1st place among 8+ competing teams.

What I Learned

I learned how important clear communication is on a team, especially when everyone has different ideas, and how to keep a design consistent when several people are building different screens.
